## PART: Vision :: Opening: stand beside the expert
### VARIANT: VIS-OPEN-v7 [candidate] (claude)
The integration of power electronics into electrical machines is one of the quiet successes of the more-electric-aircraft programme [15,16]. Integrated motor drives have moved the inverter out of the equipment bay and onto the machine housing, shortening cables, shrinking electromagnetic-interference filtering and pooling two thermal systems into one. Multilevel converters have divided the DC bus into finer voltage steps, easing device stress and moderating the dV/dt that long motor feeders turn into terminal overvoltage. Wide-bandgap devices have raised switching frequency and efficiency together and collapsed passive volume in ways silicon devices never permitted. Multiphase machines, with dual three-phase configurations now an active aerospace development line [9], have spread power across more channels to soften the fault response of the classical three-phase set. Each of these advances was won against genuine physical difficulty, and together they define the current state of the art: converters closer to machines, buses more finely divided, power more widely shared.

Across all of it, however, one structural commitment has never moved. However close the converter sits to the machine, however many levels the bus is cut into, however many phases carry the load, the converter remains an electrically distinct object that meets the machine at terminals, and the winding behind those terminals is designed to withstand the full bus voltage. The commitment is so uniform that the literature does not state it; it is simply how a motor drive is constituted, in aerospace as everywhere else. The field has optimised the distance between the two objects, the granularity of the voltage and the number of channels. It has not asked whether the partition itself, and the full-voltage winding it entails, is necessary at all. That question is where this programme begins, and answering it is the source of every result claimed below.

## PART: Vision :: The unseen line
### VARIANT: VIS-LINE-v7 [candidate] (claude)
The assumption can be stated in one sentence: the converter is a separate electrical object, and the winding therefore sees the full bus voltage. Write it down and its consequences stop looking like independent research problems and start looking like managed symptoms.

First, insulation. Because the winding sees the bus, every turn is insulated for the bus, and in kilovolt-class machines that insulation claims 20–30% of the slot cross-section [PI TO CONFIRM: source for the 20–30% slot-area figure]: volume that carries no current and produces no torque. At cruise altitude the cost becomes a failure mode. At roughly quarter-atmosphere pressure the partial-discharge inception voltage in a realistic slot geometry falls to approximately 200–300 V [13], inside the normal operating range of standard windings. A machine that passes every ground qualification can discharge continuously at altitude, eroding its insulation from the first flight with no external sign. The wide-bandgap dV/dt adopted for converter efficiency sharpens the turn-to-turn stress that drives this erosion.

Second, the gearbox, which should be stated plainly as a challenge. The pole count is wired into the winding, so the machine has one torque-speed characteristic while the propulsor needs another, and the transformation between them is performed mechanically: a gearbox sized by peak torque, oil-lubricated, wear-limited and inspection-scheduled. That is mass which does no electromagnetic work and a concentrated mechanical failure mode, doubly wrong for a system whose binding metrics are power density and reliability.

Third, multiphase redundancy, stated honestly. Multiphase machines exist precisely to soften the one-third step loss of a three-phase machine, and dual three-phase is a productive aerospace line [9]. But the softening is bought by duplicating inverter channels, gate drives, isolated supplies, sensing and protection; the added converter mass and cost dilute the very power density the redundancy defends, and the winding still sees the bus voltage and the pole count stays fixed.

Fourth, end-windings: slots are joined by copper running outside the core, the least coolable and least productive conductor in the machine. Four constraints, managed by four sub-fields; one assumption underneath all of them.

## PART: Vision :: Crossing the line: the 50 V claim
### VARIANT: VIS-CROSS-v7 [candidate] (claude)
The architectural move is direct: place one bilateral integrated-circuit pair at every stator slot, each driving its own winding section across an open-end winding, so that the 270 V bus is distributed magnetically along the winding and no section ever sees more than 20–50 V. Against the approximately 200–300 V Paschen minimum inception voltage at quarter-atmosphere [13], that envelope holds a 4–15× margin under all altitude, contamination, geometry and transient conditions. Partial discharge is eliminated because the voltage that would cause it never arrives at any insulation boundary, not because the insulation is stronger. This is not integration in the sense the motor-drive community uses the word: an integrated motor drive relocates a functionally intact inverter onto the housing and still meets the winding at full-voltage terminals. Here no such terminal exists. The IC is simultaneously the machine's per-slot excitation source and the converter's switching element, and the converter as a separate electrical object ceases to exist.

The claim is buildable now, and only now. The group has demonstrated the complete per-slot drive infrastructure, power switches, gate drivers, level shifters, dead-time generation and floating supplies, on a single 15.2 mm² die in 130 nm BCD [1]; floating-domain level shifting in the same process [2], with on-chip digital isolation demonstrated in the group's doctoral work [21]; magnetic voltage distribution with adiabatic switching at above 99% efficiency [3]; and binary-tree converters of this family driving industrial induction machines with back-EMF under closed-loop control [20]. A discrete implementation would repeat the floating-domain infrastructure roughly 36 times for 18 slots driven bilaterally, and the control hardware alone would exceed the machine volume. The architecture was not previously undiscovered; it was physically unrealisable before this silicon existed. What follows is therefore not a concept awaiting an enabling technology. The technology exists and is validated; what does not yet exist is the science of operating it inside a rotating machine.

## PART: Vision :: The cascade and its price
### VARIANT: VIS-CASC-v7 [candidate] (claude)
One decision, independently programmable current in every slot, produces the rest of the architecture's value, and each consequence is derived from it rather than added to it. Because the spatial current pattern is unconstrained, the pole count becomes a software parameter: any spatial harmonic composition can be synthesised, so the machine retunes its torque-speed characteristic to the propulsor at every operating point. The mechanical transformation a fixed-pole machine requires is no longer needed, and the gearbox, with its mass, its oil and its concentrated failure mode, leaves the drivetrain. Variable-pole drives have demonstrated the principle at terminal level through shared-bus modulation [11,12]; slot-level drive removes the shared bus and the discrete pole boundaries together.

Because the effective phase count equals the number of driven sections, six to nine in the proposed machine, fault tolerance arrives without the duplication cost that conventional multiphase pays for it. A nine-section machine retains approximately 89% of rated torque after one module failure, against approximately 58% for a three-phase machine losing one phase, and it degrades continuously rather than in a step, which is the behaviour aerospace certification expects: gradual, predictable derating. Because hairpin conductors terminate directly at the IC modules at the stator end-face, end-windings are eliminated structurally, removing the 20–40% of winding resistance they contribute and the least coolable region of the stator, while exposing the terminations at the end-face where cooling is most effective. Because the adiabatic zero-voltage switching inherited from the converter produces inherently smooth per-section voltage transitions, torque ripple falls without added filtering. And because there is no external converter, its cabling and its cabinet leave the mass budget entirely.

Nothing here is free, and the price should be stated as plainly as the benefits. The sections share one magnetic core, so N independently driven slots are not N independent actuators but N coupled ones: every slot current alters the flux, the back-EMF and the thermal environment of its neighbours. The design problem is therefore a 2N-degree-of-freedom co-design spanning winding electromagnetics, circuit operating envelope, thermal path and control, and no existing methodology addresses it: machine design, converter design and control design are separate disciplines precisely because the partition allowed them to be, and sequential design has no valid starting point, because each domain's inputs are another domain's outputs. That co-design problem, not the demonstrator machine, is the crown jewel of this programme. Whether adiabatic switching survives an inductive source with back-EMF, whether 2N-dimensional control remains stable under inter-slot coupling, and how IC modules survive thermally inside a winding environment at 120–150 °C are open questions. They are the substance of the research, not risks to it, and the framework that answers them is the programme's central and most reusable scientific contribution.

## PART: Vision :: Timeliness: the ATI collision
### VARIANT: VIS-TIME-v7 [candidate] (claude)
The enabling silicon did not exist until the current design cycle. The per-slot drive die [1] and its floating-domain infrastructure [2] were demonstrated only within the present cycle; before these demonstrations the architecture described above could not have been built at any budget. At the same moment, the demand side has arrived. The UK advanced-air-mobility sector is scaling into the 50–100 kW per-motor class where this architecture offers its greatest benefit, and both the Aerospace Technology Institute roadmap and the Jet Zero strategy call for power-density gains that incremental improvement of partitioned drives cannot deliver [18, PI TO CONFIRM: market ~£3.2 bn by 2030]. A field can hold an architectural lever long before the technology to pull it exists, or hold the technology with no strategic reason to use it; here the lever, the silicon that makes it buildable and the national requirement have arrived together. The timing matters because architectures, once committed, persist for product generations. A programme starting now establishes the design methodology in the same window in which UK airframers and propulsion integrators are fixing the architectures of their first production electric powertrains; a programme starting later documents an architecture the industry has already committed against.

## PART: Vision :: National importance
### VARIANT: VIS-NATL-v7 [candidate] (claude)
The beneficiaries are specific and can be named. Academic beneficiaries, the electrical-machines and power-electronics communities, gain the first design methodology for an N-element machine drive with 2N control degrees of freedom, reusable wherever the converter-machine partition limits performance, in aerospace and beyond it. Industrial beneficiaries, UK propulsion integrators [PI TO CONFIRM: Eaton, Airbus letters of support], gain an architecture that removes partial discharge by physics rather than by qualification, eliminates the gearbox, and degrades gracefully in a manner certification authorities can accept. Societal and economic beneficiaries gain a credible route to the lighter, more reliable electric propulsion that the Jet Zero commitment to net zero by 2050 requires, and that underpins the approximately 111,000 direct UK aerospace jobs [19, PI TO CONFIRM] dependent on national competitiveness in next-generation propulsion.

EPSRC is the right funder because no other body will fund this work. The programme operates at TRL 1–3: the architecture, the multi-physics co-design framework and bench-scale validation, with certification and flight hardware explicitly out of scope. Per-slot drive is pre-competitive, foundational infrastructure on which multiple integrators would build; it is a public good that no single firm will fund alone, because none captures enough of its value to justify paying for the underlying science, and that is precisely the class of research EPSRC exists to support. The exit is defined: a validated TRL-3 co-design framework, an open experimental dataset and a characterised demonstrator hand to industry for follow-on Innovate UK or ATI development at TRL 4–6. The knowledge this programme creates outlasts any single machine built with it; that is the return on public investment.

## PART: Vision :: The cascade and its price
### VARIANT: VIS-CASC-v8 [candidate] (claude)
Everything else follows from the same move, and each consequence can be derived rather than asserted.

Because each of the 6–9 driven sections carries an independently programmable current, the spatial harmonic content of the air-gap field is chosen in software rather than fixed in copper. The pole count becomes a control variable: any harmonic composition, at any operating point. Prior variable-pole drives established the value of this at the machine terminals over a shared bus [11,12]; per-slot drive generalises it to full spatial resolution. A machine that retunes its torque-speed characteristic to the propulsor at every operating point needs no mechanical transformation between them, so the gearbox, its oil, its inspection schedule and its failure mode leave the drivetrain.

Because the effective phase count equals the driven section count, fault tolerance arrives without the duplication that conventional multiphase pays for it. Nothing is added to obtain redundancy; the sections already exist because the architecture is made of them. A nine-section machine retains approximately 89% of rated torque after one module failure, against approximately 58% for a three-phase machine losing a phase, and it degrades continuously rather than in a step, which is the behaviour aerospace certification expects: gradual, predictable derating.

Because every conductor terminates at an IC module at the stator end-face, no copper runs outside the active magnetic length. The end-winding is eliminated structurally, removing the 20–40% of winding resistance it contributes and the least coolable region of the stator, and exposing the terminations at the end-face where cooling is most effective.

Because the switching inherited from the adiabatic converter is zero-voltage, per-section voltage transitions are smooth by construction and torque ripple falls without filtering. And because there is no external converter, its cabinet, busbars and cabling leave the mass budget entirely.

Nothing here is free, and the price deserves to be stated as plainly as the benefits. The sections share one magnetic core. N independently driven slots are therefore not N independent actuators but N coupled ones: current in any slot induces voltage in its neighbours, the back-EMF each IC faces depends on what every other IC is doing, each module rejects its heat through a winding environment at 120–150 °C, and the circuit's electrical envelope constrains the electromagnetic design that in turn sets the circuit's source conditions. The design problem is a 2N-degree-of-freedom co-design spanning electromagnetics, circuit envelope, thermal path and control, and no existing methodology addresses it; sequential design has no valid starting point, because each domain's inputs are another domain's outputs. That methodology, not the demonstrator machine, is the crown jewel of this programme: the first design science for an N-element coupled electromagnetic array, and its central scientific contribution.
