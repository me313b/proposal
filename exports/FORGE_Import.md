## PART: Summary :: Public summary (550 words)
### VARIANT: SUM-PUB-v1 [candidate] (claude)
Electric aircraft promise quieter skies and an end to aviation's fossil-fuel dependence, and the United Kingdom has committed to net zero aviation by 2050. Standing in the way is a piece of engineering architecture so old that it is rarely questioned. In every electric vehicle, train and aircraft flying today, the electric motor and the power electronics that drive it are two separate objects. The electronics create high voltages; thick insulation protects the motor's copper windings from them; and the two meet at a set of terminals. That separation, invisible to passengers, sets the limits of what electric flight can be. It forces motors to carry insulation instead of useful copper. It leaves aircraft vulnerable to an effect called partial discharge, in which the thin air at cruising altitude allows tiny sparks to gnaw invisibly through insulation that passed every test on the ground. It means a single electrical fault can remove a third of a motor's output in an instant. And because a motor's natural speed is fixed by how it is wound, aircraft must often carry a gearbox, with its oil, its inspections and its own history of serious accidents. This programme will establish the foundations of a machine in which the separation no longer exists. Instead of one large converter feeding the whole motor, a pair of tiny silicon chips sits at every one of the motor's 32 slots, each driving its own small section of winding. The high supply voltage, up to a thousand volts, is never delivered to the winding at all; it is divided magnetically along the machine itself, so that no section of copper ever experiences more than about sixteen volts. Sixteen volts cannot spark at any altitude; the physics that erodes conventional insulation simply never begins. The consequences multiply. With every slot independently controlled, the motor's characteristics become software: it can reshape itself in flight to suit the propeller, so the gearbox disappears. If one section fails, the machine loses about three per cent of its capability rather than a third, and keeps flying predictably, which is exactly what new safety rules for electric vertical take-off aircraft demand. Reliability, efficiency and simplicity improve together, because they all flow from the same design decision rather than being traded against one another. The research challenge, and the reason this needs a university programme, is that the thirty-two sections share one magnetic core and therefore influence each other. Designing and controlling sixty-four interacting quantities at once, across electromagnetics, electronics, heat and control, is a scientific problem no one has yet had to solve, because the old separation kept those disciplines conveniently apart. The team spans exactly those disciplines: the microchips already exist in prototype form and have been published, an earlier converter from the same family has already driven an industrial motor in the laboratory, and the programme will build and test a full 100-kilowatt demonstrator, the size of motor now flying on electric air-taxi prototypes. The work is supported by Eaton and Airbus, and its results, the design methods, the control theory and the measured evidence, will be published openly. If it succeeds, it gives the UK the foundations of a propulsion technology that is safer at altitude, simpler to certify, lighter to fly and cheaper to maintain than anything the old architecture allows. ## PART: Vision :: The unseen line
### VARIANT: VIS-LINE-v1 [candidate] (claude)
The partition's costs arrive as four constraint families. First, insulation and partial discharge (PD). In kilovolt-class form-wound machines only some 40 to 60 per cent of the slot is conductor; the rest is insulation and unavoidable gap [9]. At cruise altitude the problem sharpens from expensive to dangerous: partial discharge inception voltage (PDIV) roughly halves from sea level, to approximately {{PDIV_CRUISE}} near {{ALT_KPA}}, approaching the pressure-independent floor of about {{PD_FLOOR}} set by the Paschen minimum for air [5], [6]. A machine that passes every ground qualification can discharge continuously at altitude, eroding its insulation from the first flight, and fast wide-bandgap switching sharpens the turn-to-turn stress it must survive [7]. Second, the gearbox. Pole count is wired into the winding, so the machine has one torque-speed characteristic while the propulsor needs another, and the translation is mechanical: an oil system, a thirty-minute loss-of-lubrication endurance requirement and mandatory chip detection under CS-29 [29], a documented fatigue accident record [30], [31], and half to one per cent of transmitted power lost per mesh. The certified electric aircraft sector is already fleeing the component where it can: Joby abandoned gearing, Beta drives direct, and Archer's geared Midnight stands as the honest live counterexample. Third, fault fragility, and the honest price of the remedy. Three phases from one inverter lose roughly a third of output in a step when one phase opens. The field's answer is the multiphase machine [10], [11]: five phases retain about 70 per cent with one phase open, dual three-phase about 50 per cent. But the softening is bought by duplicating inverter channels, gate drives, isolated supplies, sensing and protection, diluting the power density it defends, and the winding still sits at bus voltage with its pole count fixed. Fourth, end-windings: copper outside the core, the machine's hot spot and least productive conductor [8], carrying an aspect-ratio-dependent share of winding resistance that reaches 50 per cent and more in short stacks. Four literatures, four mitigation communities, one unquestioned assumption underneath. Word count: 336.

## PART: Approach :: Research design and methodology
### VARIANT: APP-RDM-v1 [candidate] (claude)
The methodology is co-design under coupling, built and validated in three strands. The modelling strand constructs a unified state-space in which the {{SLOTS}} winding sections appear simultaneously as electromagnetic actuators, converter voltage steps, thermal sources and control channels, with inter-slot magnetic coupling retained rather than diagonalised away; model order and structure are themselves research outputs, since no existing formulation spans the four domains. The hardware strand advances on two benches: the group's existing rotating bench, on which a binary-tree converter of the same family already drives a 400 V industrial induction machine closed-loop [3], hosts early coupled-control and soft-switching experiments from month 6, de-risking the second bench, the {{SLOTS}}-slot, {{DEMO_KW}} demonstrator, which validates the framework at full scale. The measurement strand closes the loop: partial-discharge testing of winding coupons at altitude-representative pressure near {{ALT_KPA}}, calorimetric and electrical efficiency measurement, thermal imaging of in-slot modules, and fault-injection campaigns feed measured parameters back into the models so that the delivered methodology is measurement-informed rather than idealised.
