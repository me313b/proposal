## 1. What the proposal is about

**The core concept.** Every electric motor drive ever deployed keeps the power converter and the machine winding as two separate electrical objects, connected at terminals, with the winding insulated to withstand the full bus voltage. This proposal dissolves that partition. One pair of integrated circuits (ICs) sits at every stator slot, each pair driving its own winding section bilaterally across an open-end winding. The 270 V bus is not delivered to the winding and insulated against; it is **composed magnetically along the machine** (the autotransformer principle the group proved in an adiabatic converter measured above 99% efficiency), so no winding section ever sees more than 20–50 V. The IC is simultaneously the machine's per-slot excitation source and the converter's switching element; the winding conductor is simultaneously the torque-producing medium and the converter's voltage-distribution network. The converter as a separate object ceases to exist.

**Confirmed technical parameters (keep consistent everywhere):** 270 V DC bus; 20–50 V per-slot envelope; demonstrator 5–15 kW, 6,000–10,000 RPM; 18 slots; 6–9 driven sections; 12–18 IC modules (bilateral); electrical frequency 100 Hz–3 kHz; winding thermal environment 120–150 °C against a ~150–175 °C junction limit; N driven elements giving 2N control degrees of freedom; fault tolerance ~89% torque retained (9 sections, 1 failure) vs ~58% (three-phase); predecessor BTMLC IC: 15.2 mm², 130 nm BCD, 2 A, ~26 V, DC–5 kHz; DCAT converter >99% efficiency, ~400 W/in³. **Note:** the previously drafted "Paschen ~200–300 V at quarter-atmosphere / 4–15× margin" pair is under revision — see §6 item 7.

## 2. The challenges (the problem the proposal attacks)

Four constraint families, all consequences of the one unquestioned assumption (converter separate → winding sees full bus voltage):

1. **Insulation and partial discharge (PD) at altitude.** Insulation rated for the bus consumes slot area that should carry copper (in kilovolt-class form-wound machines only ~40–60% of the slot is conductor; the balance is insulation and unavoidable gaps — Hemmati et al., IEMDC 2019). At cruise altitude PD inception falls dramatically: the literature supports PDIV roughly halving from sea level (~0.7–1 kV) to ~350–500 V peak at ~20 kPa, approaching the pressure-independent ~330 V Paschen-minimum floor for air. A machine that passes every ground qualification can discharge continuously at altitude, silently eroding its insulation from the first flight. Wide-bandgap dV/dt sharpens the turn-to-turn stress. Anchor sources (verified): Lusuardi/Rumi/Cavallini/Barater/Nuzzo, IEEE Access 9:27485–27495, 2021; Meyer et al., IEEE TDEI 25(3):873–882, 2018; Madonna et al., IEEE TIA 57(2):1389–1398, 2021.
2. **The gearbox.** Pole count is wired into the winding, so the machine has one torque-speed characteristic while the propulsor needs another; the transformation is mechanical. Verified burden evidence: CS-29/FAR 29.927(c) 30-minute loss-of-lubrication endurance requirement and mandatory chip detectors; documented Super Puma planet-gear fatigue accidents (G-REDL 2009, LN-OJF 2016; 29 fatalities); 0.5–1% loss per gear mesh; drive systems ~10–15% of rotorcraft empty weight [PI TO CONFIRM: engine-inclusive]; wind-turbine gearboxes as the leading-downtime subsystem (Carroll 2016, DOI 10.1002/we.1887); the eVTOL direct-drive trend (Joby abandoned gearing; Beta direct-drive; Archer's geared Midnight is the honest live counterexample).
3. **Fault fragility, and multiphase's honest price.** Three phases from one inverter means one phase loss removes roughly a third of output in a step (three-phase has zero post-fault ride-through in standard form). The field's remedy is multiphase (canon: Levi, IEEE TIE 55(5):1893–1909, 2008, DOI 10.1109/TIE.2008.918488; Levi, TIE 63(1):433–448, 2016): five-phase retains ~70% with one open phase, six-phase 65–70% single-neutral, dual three-phase 50%. But the softening is bought by duplicating inverter channels, gate drives, isolated supplies, sensing and protection — diluting the power density it defends — and it leaves the winding at bus voltage and the pole count fixed (with one citable exception: nine-phase pole-phase modulation).
4. **End-windings.** Slots joined by copper outside the core: the machine's hot-spot and least productive conductor (Madonna et al., IEEE TIE 2019, DOI 10.1109/TIE.2018.2868288; oil-spray and potting literature evidence the severity). The end-winding share of winding resistance is aspect-ratio-dependent — up to 50–57% for short stacks, well below 20% for concentrated windings; the old flat "20–40%" claim must be presented as a range with WP1/WP4 quantifying it for this machine.

## 3. The opportunities (what dissolving the partition buys)

One decision — independently programmable current in every slot — and each benefit is **derived**, not added:

- **PD eliminated by architecture, not insulation.** 20–50 V per slot sits several times below even the ~330 V physical floor (a factor of 6.6–16), and roughly 7–25× below measured cruise-altitude PDIV. The voltage that would cause discharge never arrives at any insulation boundary. (Use the re-derived numbers once the PI approves — §6 item 7.)
- **Pole count becomes a software parameter** → any spatial harmonic composition, torque-speed retuned to the propulsor at every operating point → **the gearbox, its oil, its inspection schedule and its failure mode leave the drivetrain.** Prior variable-pole work (Chaubal et al., ITEC 2024; Libbos et al., TPEL 37(11), 2022) proved the principle at terminal level over a shared bus; per-slot drive generalises it to full spatial resolution.
- **Fault tolerance without multiphase's duplication cost**: effective phase count equals section count (6–9); nothing is added to obtain redundancy. ~89% torque retained after one module failure vs ~58% three-phase, degrading continuously — matching the certification expectation that failures be partial, bounded and predictable (EASA SC-VTOL-01 continued-safe-flight requirement; do NOT claim any standard says "gradual degradation" verbatim).
- **End-windings structurally eliminated** (hairpin conductors terminate at IC modules at the end-face) — removing an aspect-ratio-dependent share of resistance and the stator's least coolable region, exposing terminations where cooling is most effective.
- **Smooth switching**: adiabatic zero-voltage switching gives inherently smooth per-section transitions; torque ripple falls without filtering. **Mass cascade**: no external converter, cabinet, busbars or cables.
- **The crown jewel — and the price.** The sections share one magnetic core: N driven slots are N *coupled* actuators. The design problem becomes a 2N-degree-of-freedom co-design spanning winding electromagnetics, circuit envelope, thermal path and control — a problem with no existing methodology, because the partition allowed the three disciplines to stay separate. That co-design framework, not the demonstrator machine, is the programme's central scientific contribution. Open questions (= the research): does adiabatic ZVS survive an inductive back-EMF source; is 2N-DOF control stable under inter-slot coupling; how do IC modules survive thermally inside a 120–150 °C winding.
- **Why now**: the enabling silicon exists only as of the current design cycle — complete per-slot infrastructure on one 15.2 mm² die (IEEE TPEL 41(8):12842–12854, Aug 2026); floating-domain level shifting in the same 130 nm BCD process (TCAS-I, Early Access 2026); on-chip digital isolation (doctoral work); >99% adiabatic magnetic voltage distribution (DCAT, PCIM 2016); and binary-tree converters already driving a 400 V industrial induction machine under closed-loop control (doctoral work, Feb 2026 thesis). A discrete implementation would need ~36 repetitions of floating-domain infrastructure and exceeds the machine's own volume — the thesis behind the BTMLC states the scaling impossibility explicitly and lists "experimental evaluation with … inductive and motor loads" as future work. This programme is that future work, at per-slot resolution.
